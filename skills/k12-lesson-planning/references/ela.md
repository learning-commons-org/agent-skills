# ELA — lesson pedagogy

Loaded by `k12-lesson-planning` when the subject is **ELA**.

## Clarify (one question max)

Before asking anything, assess the following from all available conversation signals:

**1. State detection:** Scan the conversation for any state signal — teacher mentions a state name, uses state-specific codes (TEKS, SOL, OAS, CA-CCSS, etc.), or says "I teach in [state]." If found, store as state = [state name] and pass it as jurisdiction in the KG standard lookup. Update the default standard framework to match.

**2. Grade band.** Determine from grade level which band applies:
- **K–2**: foundational literacy — phonics/decoding OR read-aloud/comprehension (infer from standard; RF standards = phonics lesson; RL/RI standards = comprehension lesson)
- **3–5**: transitional comprehension
- **6–8**: literary and rhetorical analysis
- **9–12**: sophisticated analysis and argument

**3. Anchor text.** Note whether the teacher has specified a text. If not, select one from training knowledge appropriate to the grade and standard — or draw from the KG lesson-materials call (see learning-commons-kg.md). Flag the selection in Section 1 as suggested.

Then ask at most ONE question. Priority: (1) grade level if missing, (2) topic or text if missing, (3) lesson type for K–2 if standard doesn't clarify, (4) state if not inferrable. Infer everything else. Defaults applied silently: 45–60 min (K–2: allow 45 min), universal access design, CCSS (overridden by the detected state's framework when State Detection finds one).

### Grade band — apply before drafting

Grade band is the primary structural branch. Determine from grade level and apply the matching structure.

---

#### K–2: Foundational Literacy

Determine lesson type from the target standard:
- **RF standards** (Foundational Skills) → **Lesson Type A: Phonics/Decoding**
- **RL/RI standards** (Reading Literature / Informational) → **Lesson Type B: Comprehension via Read-Aloud**

Both types share a brief phonics review warm-up; they differ in primary focus.

**Lesson Type A — Phonics/Decoding (RF standards)**

Phases: Phonological Awareness → Phonics → Decodable Practice → Read-Aloud → Vocabulary & Discussion → Shared Writing → Exit Ticket

- **Phonological Awareness — 5–10 min**: Oral only, no print. Target the phoneme level relevant to the lesson's phonics pattern. Tasks: blending, segmenting, or phoneme manipulation. Brief, multisensory, brisk pace.
- **Phonics — 15–20 min**: Explicit phoneme-grapheme mapping of the target pattern. Teach decoding and encoding (spelling) together. Blending drills with real and nonsense words. NO guessing strategies — no picture cues, no "does that sound right?", no initial-letter prompting.
- **Decodable Practice — 10–15 min**: Students read connected text composed only of phonics patterns already taught plus known high-frequency words. NOT leveled or predictable texts. Look-fors: applying the target pattern, self-correcting by re-decoding (not by meaning-guessing).
- **Read-Aloud — 15–20 min**: Teacher reads a complex, content-rich text aloud. Purpose: build oral language comprehension, vocabulary, and content knowledge while students are still learning to decode. Select nonfiction at least 50% of the time. Questions are oral and text-dependent.
- **Vocabulary & Discussion — 5–10 min**: 2 target Tier 2 words from the read-aloud. Brief oral discussion with sentence frames.
- **Shared/Interactive Writing — 5–10 min**: Co-construct 1–2 sentences connected to the read-aloud content. Students supply phoneme-grapheme correspondences for words within their phonics knowledge.
- **Exit Ticket — 3–5 min**: One word-reading item + one word-spelling item targeting the lesson's phonics pattern.

**Lesson Type B — Comprehension via Read-Aloud (RL/RI standards)**

Phases: Phonics Warm-Up → Read-Aloud → Text-Dependent Discussion → Vocabulary → Shared Writing → Exit Ticket

- **Phonics Warm-Up — 5 min**: Brief review of a recently taught pattern. Not the lesson focus.
- **Read-Aloud — 15–20 min**: Teacher reads anchor text aloud. Stop at planned points for text-dependent oral questions. Annotation modeled by teacher on projected text if applicable.
- **Text-Dependent Discussion — 10 min**: Oral think-pair-share with sentence frames. Questions progress from general understanding → key details → vocabulary → simple inference. All students respond — every student answers, every student is heard.
- **Vocabulary — 5–10 min**: 2 Tier 2 target words from the text. Definition, context, student examples, non-examples.
- **Shared Writing — 5–10 min**: Co-construct a written response to a text-dependent question using the target vocabulary.
- **Exit Ticket — 3–5 min**: One oral or simple written comprehension question (teacher checks).

**Non-negotiables for K–2:**
- No three-cueing. Never instruct students to use picture cues, context cues, or initial letters to identify an unknown word. This directly contradicts the research and must not appear anywhere in the lesson.
- Decodable texts for phonics practice only. Leveled or predictable texts are not used as the primary decoding vehicle.
- Read-aloud is instructional. It is the vehicle for language comprehension and knowledge-building in K–2, not optional enrichment. Complex, content-rich texts must be selected and taught deliberately.

---

#### Grades 3–5: Transitional Comprehension

Phases: Launch → Close Reading → Discussion → Vocabulary → Writing Task → Exit Ticket

- **Launch — 5–10 min**: Activate prior knowledge from the unit topic. Do NOT pre-teach the text's content or answer comprehension questions in advance. One focused prompt connecting to the unit's knowledge-building arc (a question, image, or artifact). Brief.
- **Close Reading — 15–20 min**: Students read the anchor text at grade-level complexity. No text leveling. First read: gist and general understanding. Second read: assigned annotation purpose (e.g., circle evidence of a character trait / underline the author's main claim / mark unfamiliar Tier 2 words). Look-fors: 3+ specific behaviors named, what each signals, what to do.
- **Discussion — 10 min**: All students write before speaking (Think-Write-Pair-Share, every time — this is the equity move). Whole-class discussion uses text-dependent questions in this progression: general understanding → key details → vocabulary/structure → author's craft. Sentence frames provided for each question type.
- **Vocabulary — 5–10 min**: 2 Tier 2 target words from the text. Definition + text context + student example + non-example. Students will use these words in the writing task.
- **Writing Task — 10 min**: On-demand, text-dependent written response. Prompt is specific: "Using evidence from paragraphs ___ and ___, explain how the author shows…" Students must quote or paraphrase the text. Opinion writing (grades 3–4); argument with evidence (grade 5).
- **Exit Ticket — 3–5 min**: One text-dependent question requiring inference or author's craft analysis. Sort: *Got it* / *Almost there* / *Needs re-teaching*.

**Non-negotiables for 3–5:**
- Complex text for all students. If a text is too hard to comprehend, scaffold access through re-reading, discussion, and vocabulary support — not through substituting an easier text. Text difficulty is a feature, not a bug.
- Text-dependent questions only. A student who has not read the text must not be able to answer the questions from prior knowledge or personal experience alone.
- Write before speaking, every discussion. This is not optional.

---

#### Grades 6–8: Literary and Rhetorical Analysis

Phases: Launch → Close Reading → Structured Discussion → Vocabulary & Craft → Writing Task → Formative Check

- **Launch — 5–10 min**: Compelling question tied to the unit topic or text. Students write a brief response (60 seconds, not shared yet) — they will return to this at the end of the lesson to assess how their thinking has evolved.
- **Close Reading — 15–20 min**: Students annotate the anchor text with an assigned analytical lens: track how the author develops a claim / annotate craft moves (diction, structure, tone) / mark evidence for the central idea. Independent read followed by brief partner annotation comparison. 3+ look-fors named with specific student behaviors and teacher moves.
- **Structured Discussion — 15 min**: Think-Write-Pair-Share → structured whole-class discussion using text-dependent questions moving from literal comprehension to literary/rhetorical analysis. For grade 8 or strong groups: fishbowl or Socratic Seminar. Required sentence frames: "According to the text…" / "The author suggests ___, which shows…" / "I want to build on [name]'s idea — the text also…" Always debrief discussion quality, not just content.
- **Vocabulary & Craft — 5–10 min**: 1–2 Tier 2 words in depth. Plus one explicit craft focus: how does a specific word choice, structural decision, or rhetorical move serve the author's purpose? Students analyze effect, not just identify the feature.
- **Writing Task — 10–15 min**: On-demand argument or analysis paragraph. Claim + evidence + reasoning required. Grades 6–7: model the structure explicitly. Grade 8: counterclaim acknowledgment required. Include light writing scaffolds.
- **Formative Check — 3–5 min**: Students return to Launch quick-write. "Add 2–3 sentences: how has your thinking changed? What specific evidence from the text would you use now that you wouldn't have before?"

**Non-negotiables for 6–8:**
- Analysis, not summary. Writing tasks must require an arguable claim about the text — not a plot summary, not a book report. If the task can be completed without citing the text, redesign it.
- Text pairs where possible. Pairing a literary text with a related informational source makes synthesis writing possible — the highest-demand and most productive work at this grade band.
- Counterclaim in argument by grade 8. Argument that does not acknowledge and rebut an opposing view is not yet argument; it is assertion.

---

#### Grades 9–12: Rhetorical Sophistication and Sustained Argument

Phases: Launch → Close Reading → Academic Discussion → Craft & Language → Writing Task → Formative Check

- **Launch — 5 min**: A precise, genuinely arguable question about the text's central argument, rhetorical situation, or thematic claim. Students write independently — no whole-class discussion yet. Silence is productive.
- **Close Reading — 15–20 min**: Students annotate for a specific analytical lens (e.g., how the author builds ethos through diction / how syntactic choices create urgency / how structure contributes to argument). Multiple reads with different purposes if the passage is short. Complexity is the point — do not simplify or excerpt around difficult passages.
- **Academic Discussion — 15 min**: Student-to-student. Teacher probes; does not direct. For Socratic Seminar: genuinely debatable open question; students must reference specific passages by paragraph or line. Debrief on both content and discussion quality: "Did we push on the hardest question? Whose idea did someone else build on?" Language Analysis option: unpack one particularly complex or well-crafted sentence (10–15 min; word choice, syntax, meaning — appropriate for supporting MLLs and deepening language comprehension for all).
- **Craft & Language — 5–10 min**: One precision craft move (diction, syntax, structure, tone, imagery, narrative distance, rhetorical appeals). Students analyze effect and author intent — not identification alone.
- **Writing Task — 15–20 min**: Sustained analytical or argumentative response (minimum 1 substantial paragraph; extended writing or essay begins here). Literary analysis: arguable claim + textual evidence + analysis of craft. Argument: claim + evidence + reasoning + counterclaim acknowledgment. Include writing scaffolds.
- **Formative Check**: Return to Launch quick-write. Students evaluate their own thinking: "Assess the argument you wrote at the start. What evidence would you now add? What would you revise?"

**Non-negotiables for 9–12:**
- Analysis of *how* and *why*, not just *what*. A student who identifies a metaphor has not done analysis. A student who explains what effect that metaphor creates for a specific audience in a specific rhetorical context has.
- No text avoidance. Do not excerpt around difficult passages; do not substitute contemporary summaries for original texts; do not pre-explain meaning before students grapple with the text independently.
- Argument requires counterclaim by grade 11, and rhetorical analysis requires attention to audience, purpose, and context — not just stylistic features in isolation.

### Section structure — all grade bands

1. **At a Glance** — standard verbatim in a `special` callout (the ONE verbatim quote — everywhere else standards go by code + ≤10-word gist); anchor text (title + genre + Lexile if known, or [suggested] flag); materials list
2. **Learning Goal** — Big Idea (enduring understanding, 1 sentence tied to the text and unit); SWBAT bullets drawn from KG learning components (learning-commons-kg.md, ELA call 2), naming specific sub-skills; Prerequisite (prior standard by code + gist + 1 sentence on prior knowledge assumed)
3. **Vocabulary & Anticipated Challenges** — 2–3 Tier 2 target words with definitions and text context; 3 misconceptions specific to this text and task, drawn from the KG or training knowledge, each formatted: *What students do* / *Why it happens* / *Teacher move*
4. **Rationale** — 3 non-negotiables specific to this grade band and standard, each with a 1–2 sentence rationale grounded in the research (cite Science of Reading for K–2 phonics lessons; cite text complexity/HQIM principles for 3–12)
5. **Lesson Sequence** — phases per grade band above; in every reading phase: 3+ look-fors each naming the specific student behavior, why it matters for the standard, and what to do; in every discussion phase: specific text-dependent prompts (not generic) + 2–3 sentence frames

→ **Section structure complete. Proceed immediately to Step 4 to generate output files.**

## Exit ticket guidance

The exit ticket is the last phase in Lesson Sequence (`from_shared:exit_ticket` under its phase header). One item targeting the hardest inference or application the standard requires. K-2 phonics: word-reading + word-spelling. K-2 comprehension: oral or simple written check. 3-12: text-dependent question requiring evidence; 6-12 may instead use a return-to-the-Launch-quick-write self-assessment. Three sort buckets (Got it / Almost there / Needs re-teaching).
